# Evolutio

A local TypeScript / Three.js artificial-life island. Run `npm install`, then `npm run dev`. `npm run build` checks TypeScript and builds the app; `npm test` checks simulation, inheritance, rendering, families, ecology and saves.

## Life and evolution

Mice and cats have live young. Babies follow their recorded parent, and a nearby guarding parent reduces predation damage while spending energy. Reproduction remains one-parent inheritance, not sexual reproduction or a mother/father pair.

Bugs, birds, snakes, fish, turtles, crocodiles and frogs lay eggs. Clutch sizes and incubation times differ: bugs/fish/frogs lay eight, snakes six, turtles/crocodiles four, birds two. Eggs contain already-mutated genomes and ancestry. Water eggs (fish/frog) need water; other eggs need dry ground. Environmental damage and a species-dependent hatching survival chance mean many eggs fail. This is a simplified reproductive strategy, not a species-accurate egg count or development model.

Birds inherit an insect-eating or raptor role. Insect eaters eat bugs and worms; raptors hunt mice and fish. Birds take off to travel or flee and descend to eat/drink. Ground predators cannot catch high-flying birds. Flight costs energy. Fish feed on a replenishing aquatic resource; turtles also graze plants; frogs eat bugs/worms; crocodiles hunt fish, frogs, mice and birds. Aquatic animals seek water; fish can die when stranded. Each species has a distinct procedural body and gait.

Snakes have body temperature with gradual warming/cooling, basking behaviour, temperature-dependent speed and reduced energy/water use when cold. The numerical curve is a gameplay approximation, informed by [ectotherm thermoregulation](https://openstax.org/books/biology/pages/29-4-reptiles), not species-specific physiology.

Inherited traits include tiny neural networks, speed, senses, activity, aggression, body shape, gait, camouflage, eyesight, digging, preferred moisture, drought tolerance, swimming and immunity. Adaptations have upkeep costs. Food, thirst and danger can override habitat preferences. Plants inherit growth, defence, spread, drought/flood tolerance and disease resistance.

## Changing island

A 70-unit-radius island supports forests, plains, deserts, lakes and a river. A saved, interpolated 20×20 field tracks soil moisture, woodland, grass, litter, fungi and worms. Rain, evaporation, seasonal drying and nearby water change it gradually. Ground colours show woodland succession and desertification. Plants lose health under drought, flooding or disease and brown before dying. Mature plants deposit dormant seeds that can germinate after rain, preserving lineages without adding animal founders.

Wet litter supports visible mushrooms and decomposer worms. Fungi recycle a small growth benefit but dense damp patches can produce infection; animals also transmit infection through close contact. Immune animals resist/recover faster. Worms and mushrooms are simplified patch resources, not individually evolving creatures. Pond food is a bounded shared aquatic resource. River inflow and near-water soil recharge approximate an outside catchment; this is not a closed hydrological model.

Animals seek water, shelter and suitable habitats. Breath runs out during prolonged submersion for terrestrial animals; aquatic species use simplified water tolerance. Fish lose breath out of water. Recorded animal death causes include predation, exhausted energy, dehydration, exposure, drowning, stranding, disease and old age. Plant losses are counted separately. Extinction remains possible.

## Watching and saving

Click an animal for its brain, parent, offspring and generation-to-generation trait changes. Camera following preserves orbit/zoom; family rings identify parent and offspring. The inspector shows infection, breath, local conditions, pending eggs, bird diet/altitude and snake temperature. The journal shows regional weather, eggs, plant losses and species counts. Use **Introduce missing species** once on an older island to add new aquatic species or missing bird roles without resetting it. This is manual; there are no automatic animal replacements.

Save format v16 includes terrain history, all organisms/genomes, incubating eggs, seed bank, disease, ponds, dens, random state and simulation time. Older saves migrate without replacing existing organisms. Saves stay in the same browser/site address and resume without offline simulation. Autosave runs every ten seconds and on hide/close. Reset replaces the island after confirmation.

The cap is 560 living animals plus eggs, with per-species quotas, and 2,000 plants. Bodies use instanced low-poly geometry. Run `node --experimental-strip-types scripts/balance.mjs 42 1800 0.25` to produce a reproducible 30-minute balance report under `reports/`; the normal app uses 0.1-second steps. Long-run coexistence depends on seed and conditions; caps and simplified survival instincts deliberately help this game remain watchable.


Local cloud patches and soft ground shadows expose the same spatial weather field used by plant growth and animal warmth. Sunshine and temperature vary smoothly across the map. Underlying aridity persists independently of current vegetation: desert soil gets less rain, drains faster and retains less water, while nearby rivers/lakes support oases. Desert boundaries can move and briefly green after rain without uniform woodland replacing the whole map.

Arrow keys pan relative to the camera; Shift pans faster. Manual panning disengages animal follow and ignores input fields and open dialogs. Trees have larger canopies. Rocks offer cover and sun-warmed basking surfaces. Young animals and elders have lower agility and higher predation vulnerability than adults. Snakes seek rock/den nest sites or bury a shallow clutch, spend energy and time nesting, and gain a concealment survival benefit; flooding still damages the eggs.


Cats, foxes and wolves use a hunger-driven rest/stalk/sprint/feed cycle. Cats have short explosive bursts; foxes sit between cats and wolves; wolves have longer stamina reserves. A chase drains stamina and forces recovery; well-fed hunters rest with lower upkeep. Attacks cost energy and only a kill creates a finite catch, eaten over time at its location. A catch expires after 90 simulated seconds; predators may leave it to escape or drink and return while it remains. Stamina, recovery and remaining food persist in saves and appear in the inspector. Foxes and wolves have live young and the same parental protection system. They are distinct species; pack coordination is not modeled. Older islands can introduce them using the existing introduction control. Predator caps share the island's existing 560-animal-and-egg limit.


Body needs now govern rest and urgency across species: exhausted animals recover, well-fed animals conserve energy, and urgent hunger can interrupt comfort-seeking. Thirst, immediate danger and escaping water override rest. Maturation, longevity and endurance are inherited and mutate; longer life, rapid development and endurance have metabolic costs. Each species has its own compressed game-time maturity/lifespan baseline, from bugs (6/160 seconds) to crocodiles (90/1800 seconds), modified by genes and body size. Fertility runs from maturity to 85% of inherited lifespan. Growth, agility, parental protection and old-age death use these individual life histories. Older living saves retain their relative life stage when migrated; recorded historical ages remain unchanged.


Lakes spill through temporary channels when their water level exceeds 86%. Outlet flow rises and decays gradually, drains lake water and widens/deepens the river. The river reaches the island edge and drops into the surrounding water, with animated currents, a waterfall and mist. Pond evaporation plus a simplified river/mist contribution supply the cloud cycle; fronts drift inland from the waterfall side. This remains a lightweight open-catchment model, not a volume-conserving fluid solver.

Five smooth river fords are narrower and shallower. Land animals can plan crossings to reach food or other needs, trading travel distance against width, depth and inherited swimming ability. Water contact increases wetness, fatigue, heat loss and energy use; drying is gradual and faster in sunshine. Dangerous crossings can still exhaust or drown animals. Active lake outlet channels are water for swimming and flood stress; plants cannot be placed in them. Outlet flow, wetness and crossing plans are saved.



The island now has a 70-unit radius (2.53 times the previous area). Animal and egg limits remain 560 with unchanged species caps; plant capacity grows to 2,000 (1,000 grass, 550 bushes, 450 trees). Version 17 saves preserve the original land cells, organisms, lakes and homes and seed vegetation only in the newly available outer land. Weather uses a 20×20 grid at the same spatial resolution.

Insulation and water resistance are inherited, mutable traits. Water resistance reduces soaking from rain and wading; insulation reduces wet cooling and cold exposure but increases heat stress. Both cost maintenance energy. Wet land animals seek locally sunny rocks or cover/finished family dens, rest to dry and reduce exposure, while thirst, starvation risk, predators and water escape remain higher priorities.


Performance model: rendering is capped at 30 fps; organism meshes and environmental instances refresh only after simulation changes, selection changes or organism additions/removals; shadows refresh with them. Simulation remains fixed at 0.1 seconds per tick. A bounded backlog and a 6 ms inter-step budget stop accelerated speeds monopolizing a frame; a single step may exceed that budget. The journal reports actual versus requested speed. On overloaded devices simulation slows instead of accumulating unlimited catch-up work. Hidden tabs do not render or simulate.

Food/threat sensing runs every 1–5 ticks: chases each tick, resting animals and turtles every fifth, cold snakes every fourth. Immediate nearby threats, hunger changes and invalid targets trigger early sensing. Bodies, movement, neural steering, collisions and damage still update each tick; this is scheduled perception, not frozen animals. Cached targets and schedules are saved in version 18. Clouds, soil and pond/river state integrate at one-second simulation gates, preserving partial intervals across saves. Sunlight and visual motion remain time-derived.

A local 200-step Node sample (seed 7823, 100 warm-up steps, about 2,000 plants) fell from 21.5 ms/step to 13.8 after terrain interpolation changes and 9.5 after adaptive sensing, before weather gating. These are development measurements under varying machine load, not mobile-browser guarantees. Reproduce with `node --experimental-strip-types scripts/profile.mjs`; CPU profiles are in reports/. Next trade-offs to measure: plant growth at slower staggered intervals, distant mesh detail/shadows, then moving simulation to a Worker if main-thread steps remain too long. WASM is not required for this first optimization pass.


For opt-in live CPU diagnostics, open `?profile=1` and expand Performance diagnostics at the bottom of the journal. The panel separates simulation phases, organism/environment mesh preparation, UI and rendering submission (not GPU execution time). Run `node --experimental-strip-types scripts/profile.mjs` for a mature-world benchmark and CPU profile. See `reports/performance-review.md` for measured bottlenecks and ranked trade-offs.


Version 19 staggers plant growth, infection, health recovery and reproduction across ten ID-based cohorts, once per simulated second. Age and grazing remain tick-based; critically ill plants (health at or below 1) get immediate health checks. Each plant saves its last growth time so partial intervals survive reloading. One-second integration is an approximation and can change long-term lineages compared with 0.1-second growth.

Water queries cache pond radii and nearby river cross-sections, invalidating immediately when water level, flow or geometry changes; far dry land bypasses detailed river calculations. Cover caches retain exact-position results only while vegetation membership and landscape snapshots remain unchanged. No shoreline or cover coordinates are rounded. Plant meshes refresh at most twice per real second unless plants are added/removed or a plant is selected; animal meshes retain their normal cadence. Dormant seed validation now uses the expanded island bounds.

`node --experimental-strip-types scripts/compare-performance.mjs` alternates three before/after trials from the same mature saved fixture, with a separate fixed-population mesh replay. The frozen baseline is in `reports/perf-baseline/src`; its save validator has only the outer-island seed bound repair applied so the common fixture loads. Results are in `reports/performance-comparison.json`.


Animal-loop optimization retains sensing cadence and per-tick body/damage updates. It skips water-site selection for hydrated animals, indexes dens/counts, shares wide sensing candidates, uses early-exit spatial queries, replaces rock/refuge sorting with single-pass selection, and reuses within-tick climate at drying sites. Close food can now wake a hungry animal’s scheduled scan, alongside close predators and invalid targets. Reproduce matched pre/post measurements with `scripts/compare-animal-performance.mjs`; see `reports/animal-optimization-results.md`.

Version 20 adds nine plant varieties: grass tufts, ferns, wildflowers, leafy shrubs, berry bushes, cacti, conifers, broadleaf trees and acacias. Cactus/acacia founders and desert-born plants start with high inherited drought tolerance and lower flood tolerance; drought tolerance also reduces drought damage. They still need water and can die during prolonged drought. Older islands keep their existing genes and gain deterministic visual varieties.

Time controls are separate from Shape & nurture tools. Click the island to apply adjustable water, sand, soil, plant, disease, mushroom, raise or dig brushes; removal reverses the corresponding effect. Water moistens soil and refills existing lakes rather than creating new lakes. Mushrooms have caps/stems and can be selected to inspect their fungal soil patch. Gentle hills and elevation edits are visual terrain: river corridors stay flat and this does not add slope physics or a fluid solver. Terrain edits and plant varieties persist in saves.



Version 22 connects terrain to a coarse shallow-water field on the existing 8 m landscape grid. Rain adds water, soil absorbs it, evaporation removes it, and hydraulic-head differences transfer it downhill in four conservative substeps per simulated second. River/lake boundaries exchange with connected depressions; water drains at the coast. Digging an isolated hole does not create water. Use broad connected trenches (roughly 8–16 m brushes) and rain or the Water brush to fill them. The rendered field and animal wetness/drinking/flooding queries share surface-water sampling. This is a gameplay approximation, not a fluid solver: small trenches below grid resolution and precise river diversion are not represented. Existing river/lake reservoirs remain boundary sources; their historical aquatic food remains abstract rather than individual aquatic plants.

Plants have no simulation count or family cap. Local vegetation demand, tree shade, soil water, rainfall, maintenance and finite lifespans limit growth/reproduction, and crowding can kill plants. Rendering instance buffers expand on demand. The save decoder's 100,000-entry sanity guard protects against malformed saves; it is not a growth rule. On a seed-7823 sample, the populated island had 1,937 plants after 60 simulated seconds and 2,498 after 240; without animals it reached 3,050 at 180 seconds, then varied between 2,450 and 2,968 at the later 180-second samples through 900 seconds. Different seeds, climates, inherited traits and user edits yield different equilibria; 2,000 is an approximate play balance, not a guaranteed target. The no-animal run used 1 s steps and the populated run 0.1 s, so these are illustrative trajectories rather than a controlled grazing comparison.

Mice, bugs and insect-eating birds forage finite fungal cover, gaining energy/water and reducing the patch. Trees are taller relative to animals; shrubs, grasses and especially mushroom caps are smaller. Dead plants retain brown shrinking foliage, bare stems/branches and ground litter; woody remains tip over and shrink as they decompose. Wet/fungal ground speeds decay, and decomposition returns litter to the soil. Remains are inspectable, saved, and excluded from living plant counts and food targets.

The time controls stay in the lower left. Day/night presentation adds a blue daylight sky, sun, drifting clouds, cool night lighting, moon and cloud-dimmed stars through a lightweight decorative sky layer; no extra shadow maps are required. Saved worlds from previous versions migrate without resetting. Run `npm run test:quick` for focused hydrology, resource, diet, decay, save and rendering-buffer checks, plus the existing simulation regression tests.


Version 23 adds inherited daily rhythms: activity phase (a circular daily clock), dawn/dusk preference and active-window width. Species start with broad gameplay tendencies, including daytime birds/turtles, nighttime mice/frogs, and twilight-biased cats/wolves. Founders vary and children mutate all three traits; phase mutations wrap across midnight. These are preferences rather than rigid schedules. An inactive animal seeks a nearby finished den or vegetation, or rests in place; birds land first. Sleeping stops movement, restores stamina and reduces maintenance energy use without stopping ageing, weather exposure or danger checks. Hunger wakes sleepers below 55 energy and they need more than 85 energy to settle again; thirst, nearby predators, dangerous water, nesting and severe weather take precedence. Full feeding/rest behavior still applies during active hours. The compact inspector shows Sleeping/Asleep, the inherited rhythm and whether it is currently active/rest hours; the expanded trait comparison includes all three genes. Older saves migrate living animals, archived relatives and egg embryos without advancing the simulation or changing the random seed.


Version 24 makes sleeping visible with curled mammals, coiled snakes, tucked turtles, folded-wing/head-tucked birds and low resting poses for other species. Breathing follows simulation age, so poses freeze while paused. Birds remember a living mature tree, approach a shared simulation/render branch position, and settle in a visible nest; perched sleep has no flight upkeep. Removing the tree invalidates the roost, and birds find another tree or use a simple ground-nest fallback. Other animals prefer their completed dry den, can adopt a nearby completed den, and land animals avoid sampled deep-water routes to it. Birds do not use underground dens. The compact inspector names the tree nest, ground nest or nearby den while sleeping. Roost references migrate and persist with save version 24. `src/resting.test.ts` checks all eleven poses, branch arrival, nest picking, den selection, flooded routes, lost trees and saving. `/reports/sleep-poses.html` is a static review gallery using the actual instanced game meshes and no saved island.

Bird broods now reuse tree nests (or ground nests when no tree is available). Parents return to feed and guard chicks using their own energy and water; mammals born in completed dens stay there for their early growth. Nursery departure is tied to inherited maturity: birds fledge at 65% of maturity, mammals leave at 40%, then follow parents until adulthood. Urgent danger, flooding, lost shelter or severe deprivation can force departure. Dependent broods delay further reproduction. Save version 25 preserves nursery locations and migrates existing islands.

Family care is visible: returning parents carry a mouthful representing spare energy, hungry nursery young beg, and real transfers trigger feeding and chewing poses. Animations pause with the simulation. Save version 26 retains feeding cues and migrates older islands. The island overlay now explains selectable wildlife and nurseries instead of assigning fixed colours to diets.

## Code organization and checks

See [the architecture review](docs/architecture-review.md) for module ownership, prioritized follow-up work, and validation limits. Shared model types, species settings, biology rules, brain evaluation, nursery care and the app shell now have separate modules. `sim.ts` retains compatibility exports. Animal states are checked by TypeScript and the save loader from one shared list.

Run `npm run typecheck`, `npm run format:check`, `npm run test:quick`, and `npm run build` during development. `npm test` also runs the longer stability checks. Tests are discovered automatically; add new `*.test.ts` files under `src`.

## Species profiles and future levels

Animals, plants and fungi now use shared family profiles with species/variety overrides. The current island is registered as the sandbox level; its initial conditions and behavior are preserved. Level definitions support seeds, initial climate/populations, available animal species, tools and objectives, with progress stored separately from ecosystem state. Version 27 saves migrate older islands to sandbox without resetting them. See [profiles and levels](docs/profiles-and-levels.md) for extension examples, supported terrain, and save compatibility rules. No level-selection screen or new challenge levels have been added yet.
